# Develop a post angle

Deliberately small. The blog is supposed to cost ~1–2 hr/week, so this skill's job
is to make sure the hour he does spend goes at something worth writing — not to
generate a content calendar.

Read `AGENTS.md` § Positioning and § Voice first, then read
`../plain-writing/SKILL.md`. Voice is a hard requirement: plain, casual, easy to
read, no unexplained jargon.

Two files to read before proposing anything new:

- **`../../../ROADMAP.md`** (repo root) — the sequenced plan. If he asks what to
  write next, the answer is almost always "the next thing in the roadmap." Say so
  rather than generating a fresh idea.
- **[backlog.md](backlog.md)** — the standing idea list with the critique each has
  already received. The answer is often "you already have a sharper version of
  this idea."

## Procedure

### 1. Establish the angle

If he named a topic, work with it. If he asked what to write, pull from
`backlog.md` and recommend **one**, with the reason.

Then pressure-test it before doing any outlining:

- **Which positioning legs does it stand on?** Needs at least two of capital
  allocation, technology landscape, operational execution. One leg means it's a
  post someone else could write.
- **What's the information edge?** What does he know that a smart generalist
  writing the same post doesn't? Plaid, Meta, Houlihan, the portfolio, the repos.
  No edge, no post — an opinion on something he has no special view into
  subtracts credibility.
- **Is there a number, a chart, or a table?** These are what get forwarded. A post
  that's all prose usually means the thesis isn't sharp yet.
- **Is the claim falsifiable?** "AI is changing finance" is a topic. "The only
  thing AI is replacing in corporate finance is the spreadsheet" is a claim.

If it fails these, say so directly and propose the sharper adjacent version. That
is more useful than an outline of a weak idea.

### 2. Produce the angle

Short. This is a working document, not a deliverable:

- **Title** — the claim, not the topic.
- **Thesis** — one sentence he'd defend at dinner.
- **Hook** — the opening two sentences, written out. If you can't write a hook,
  the angle isn't ready.
- **Outline** — 3–5 sections, one line each.
- **Evidence needed** — what he has to look up, compute, or pull from a repo.
  Be specific; this is the real cost of the post and he should see it before
  committing.
- **The asset** — the chart, table, or matrix, if there is one.
- **Effort** — a rough hour count, honestly.

### 3. Stop

Hand him the angle and stop. Do not draft the post unless he asks. If he does,
switch registers per `AGENTS.md` § Voice and write it — but the outline is the
deliverable here.

## Never

- Generate a batch of ideas he didn't ask for, or propose a posting cadence.
- Recommend a listicle, a "10 lessons from" framing, or a "manifesto".
- Propose a post on something he has no information edge in.
- Inflate the effort estimate downward to make an idea look attractive.
